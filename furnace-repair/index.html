From the hallway, a furnace that will not heat looks the same no matter what broke. Inside the cabinet, though, everything happens in a strict order: inducer on, pressure switch closed, igniter glowing, gas valve open, burners lit, flame proven, and finally the blower sending heat through the house. Diagnosis is mostly a matter of finding where that chain breaks. In Delray Beach homes, it tends to be the same handful of culprits: a flame sensor coated in oxide, a hot surface igniter cracked by thermal cycling, a pressure switch held open by a tired inducer or a plugged condensate trap, or a control board with a burned relay.

Rather than guess, Delray Beach HVAC tests the sequence one stage at a time. We read igniter resistance, measure flame signal in microamps, check that the pressure switch responds to real inducer draft, confirm voltage at the gas valve, and test the blower and its capacitor. Because this is a fuel-burning appliance, the safety basics come with every visit: the heat exchanger gets inspected, gas connections get leak-tested, and draft gets verified. You cannot tell a furnace problem from a furnace hazard by looking at the thermostat. Before we leave, temperature rise is measured against the data plate so the fire and the airflow are balanced as the manufacturer intended.

Conventional or condensing, gas, propane, or electric, we repair them all, across every major brand, including the traps, drains, and dual pressure switches that come with high-efficiency models. The parts most likely to fail are already stocked on the truck: igniters, flame sensors, pressure switches, capacitors, inducer assemblies, and blower motors. Warning Signs for Furnace Repair

Spotting the early warning signs gives you the chance to act before the situation spirals into something far more expensive. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Water, rust stains, or black soot around the flue or cabinet
  • Clicking and a glowing igniter with no burner flame
  • A furnace that shuts off every few minutes before the house warms up
  • A flame that burns soft and yellow rather than steady blue
  • A fan that keeps running while the registers blow cool air
  • Loud booms at startup caused by delayed ignition
  • Squealing or grinding from the blower compartment
  • Repeated shutdowns just seconds after the burners light

Why Hire a Professional for Furnace Repair?

A furnace combines gas, flame, high voltage, and a flue in one cabinet, and the failure that matters most is the one you cannot see from outside: a compromised heat exchanger passing combustion gases into the air your blower pushes through the house. Delayed ignition that booms at light-off stresses that exchanger further every morning it goes unfixed. Meanwhile the diagnostic traps stack up: a furnace that trips its high limit may have a bad switch, or it may be protecting itself from a clogged filter or a dying blower, and replacing the switch in the second case just removes a safety while leaving the fault.

Our experienced technicians treat every symptom as a question about the whole system. Flame signal gets measured rather than assumed, so a healthy sensor is never replaced while a cracked igniter or weak gas valve skates by. Draft is verified at the pressure switch and the flue. Temperature rise is checked against the data plate, which is how airflow problems get caught before they shorten the exchanger's life. And the gas side is leak-tested with instruments, not a sniff and a shrug. The result is a furnace that has been proven safe and correct under a full cycle, and an honest report if the unit is nearing the point where replacement beats another repair.

Furnace Repair Prevention Tips

A little routine maintenance goes a long way toward avoiding a mid-season breakdown. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Keep a working carbon monoxide alarm on every level of the home and check it each month
  • Notice how the furnace sounds at startup, because a quiet light-off is healthy and a boom is not
  • Put in a fresh filter monthly through the heating season, the cheapest protection your furnace can get
  • Keep storage, lint, and especially chlorine-based products away from the furnace closet
  • Have the furnace checked every autumn, while a weak igniter or fouled sensor is still an easy fix
  • Make sure the furnace has clear combustion air; a packed utility room starves the burners
  • If you have a high-efficiency furnace, check that the condensate drain is flowing freely
