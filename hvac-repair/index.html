Warning Signs for HVAC Repair

Spotting the early warning signs gives you the chance to act before the situation spirals into something far more expensive. Keep an eye out for the following clues that it's time to bring in an expert:

  • Warm air from the vents in cooling mode, or cool air in heating mode
  • Grinding, squealing, buzzing, or banging you have not heard before
  • Utility bills climbing while comfort keeps falling
  • A humming outdoor unit that never kicks on
  • Frequent on-off cycling while the house never gets to the thermostat setting
  • Puddles near the furnace or air handler from a blocked drain line
  • Weak airflow from the supply registers throughout the house
  • A circuit breaker that keeps tripping when the system starts

The Benefits of Professional HVAC Repair

Two things make HVAC repair a poor DIY project: the diagnosis is misleading, and the equipment is dangerous. On the diagnostic side, a weak capacitor, a low charge, a frozen evaporator coil, and choked airflow can all produce a system that runs without cooling, so trial-and-error part swaps get costly in a hurry. On the safety side, you are dealing with 240-volt circuits, capacitors that stay charged after the breaker is off, federally regulated refrigerant that requires recovery equipment, and, on gas furnaces, fuel lines, combustion, and venting where a single mistake can put gas or carbon monoxide into the home.

What professional service buys you is certainty. Instead of guesses, our technicians use meters on capacitors and motors, superheat and subcooling readings on the refrigerant side, microamp flame signal tests before any board is condemned, and static pressure measurements when the duct system may be starving the equipment. That usually makes the repair cheaper as well as safer. And because an aging unit is not always worth fixing, we give you a straight answer on repair versus replacement before you commit to either one.

How to Prevent HVAC Repair Problems

A few simple habits now, like regular filter changes and seasonal tune-ups, can add years to your system's life. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Give the condenser room to breathe by keeping plants, leaves, and clippings away from it
  • Swap in a fresh filter every one to three months; a clogged one starves the whole system of air
  • Call about unusual noises early, since a squealing bearing costs far less to fix before the motor seizes
  • Book a professional tune-up in spring for cooling and in fall for heating
  • Flush the condensate drain line each spring before algae can clog it and trip the float switch
  • Ease the strain in extreme weather by nudging the thermostat a few degrees toward the outdoor temperature
  • Leave every supply and return vent open and free of furniture or rugs
